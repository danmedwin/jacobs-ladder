# The angel’s visit: the script

A readable copy of what the tool says, for review. It is generated from `voices/data/angel.js`, so mark changes here and they will be carried into the data.

Small gray references are the source cards a teacher can open during the visit. Numbered lines are the steps the teacher moves through with Next.

## Grades K–2

*Short lines, pictures, and moving around. The teacher reads aloud.*

1. Hello! I’m an angel. In Hebrew, an angel is a *malach*. That means “messenger.”
2. I was on the ladder in Jacob’s dream! <sub>Genesis 28:12</sub>
3. *[Animation: Stained Glass, from the Jacob’s Dream gallery.]*
4. Lots of angels were going up and down, up and down. <sub>Genesis 28:12</sub>
5. **Everybody moves: Fly like an angel!** Stretch your arms out like wings. Float up high on your tiptoes… now float down low. Up… and down!
6. Do you know why we were going up and down? We wanted to see Jacob! <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
7. First, we went up to heaven to look at a picture of Jacob there. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
8. Then we came down the ladder to see the real Jacob. And there he was… fast asleep! <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
9. **Everybody moves: Shh! He’s sleeping!** Finger on your lips: shhh! Tiptoe down the ladder, very, very quietly.
10. **The angel asks:** Why do you think the angels wanted to see Jacob?
   - Button **“He was special”** → Yes! The Sages said Jacob’s picture was up in heaven because God was so proud of him. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
   - Button **“To keep him safe”** → We did want him to be safe. And God was right there with him, too.
   - Button **“They were curious”** → We were! We wanted to see the real Jacob, not just his picture.
   - *Teacher: Let everyone answer out loud. Then choose what most of the group said.*
11. Then God was right there beside Jacob. God said, “I am with you. I will keep you safe wherever you go.” <sub>Genesis 28:13; Genesis 28:15</sub>
12. **The angel asks the group to discuss:** Who helps keep you safe?
   - *Teacher: Take a few answers out loud. Then tap to hear the angel.*
   - Then the angel answers: Your grown-ups, your teachers, your friends… And Jacob learned that God is with you, too, wherever you go.
13. **The angel:** What would you like to ask me?
   1. *Do you have wings?* → The Torah doesn’t say what the angels on the ladder looked like! Artists have imagined us in lots of ways. / *[Pictures: Origami, Blown Glass, 3D Animated, Goldwork Embroidery.]* Here’s how some artists pictured us.
   2. *How many angels were there?* → The Torah just says “angels.” The Talmud says there were at least four: two going up and two coming down, all at the same time! <sub>Chullin 91b · the ladder</sub>
   3. *How big was the ladder?* → Enormous! The Talmud says angels are huge, and the ladder was wide enough for four of us to pass each other. <sub>Chullin 91b · the ladder</sub>
   4. *Why did you go up first?* → Good question! A teacher named Rashi noticed that too. He said the angels who took care of Jacob in the Land of Israel went up, and new angels came down to take care of him on his trip. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 28:12</sub>
14. **The rung:** “Up and down, God is all around!”
   - **The motion:** Climb with your hands: up, up, up… and down, down, down.
15. Thanks for visiting me! Who do you want to meet next? *(Suggests: Jacob, The stone, Rebekah.)*

## Grades 3–4

*Short lines, choices, and puzzles. Students read along.*

1. Shalom! I’m one of the angels from Jacob’s dream. In Hebrew, I’m a *malach*, a messenger.
2. Jacob dreamed of a ladder set on the ground with its top reaching the sky, and angels of God going up and down on it. That was us! <sub>Genesis 28:12</sub>
3. *[Animation: Persian Miniature, from the Jacob’s Dream gallery.]*
4. Here’s a secret about the last word of that verse, *bo*. It can mean “on it,” on the ladder. It can also mean “on him,” on Jacob. <sub>Genesis 28:12</sub>
5. **The angel asks:** Two rabbis, Rabbi Chiya and Rabbi Yannai, argued about it. Were the angels going up and down on the ladder, or up and down because of Jacob?
   - Button **“On the ladder”** → That’s the simple reading, and one of the rabbis said so. But listen to what the other rabbi said.
   - Button **“Because of Jacob”** → That’s what one of the rabbis said! Listen to why.
   - Button **“Both”** → The Sages liked to keep both answers. Now listen to the surprising one.
   - *Teacher: Let students argue for a minute before anyone chooses.*
6. He said that Jacob’s picture was carved up in heaven. So we angels went up to look at it… <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
7. …and then we came down to see the real Jacob. And we found him fast asleep on a rock! <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
8. **The angel asks the group to discuss:** Jacob had just tricked his father and run away from home. Why would his picture be up in heaven?
   - *Teacher: Take a few answers. Then tap to hear the angel.*
   - Then the angel answers: The Sages quoted God’s words in the book of Isaiah: “Israel, in whom I glory.” God was proud of Jacob, even on his worst night. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub> / Maybe God could see who Jacob would become, not just what he had done.
9. Then God was standing beside Jacob, and said, “Remember, I am with you: I will protect you wherever you go.” <sub>Genesis 28:13; Genesis 28:15</sub>
10. The Talmud tells the story a little differently, and we angels don’t look so good in it. The angels who looked at Jacob’s picture wanted to harm him! Rashi says they were jealous. So right away, God stood over Jacob to protect him. <sub>Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>
11. Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said that if the Torah didn’t say it, we would never dare to: God stood over Jacob like a parent waving over their child. Rashi explains that the parent is waving a fan, to protect the child from the heat. <sub>Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>
12. **The angel asks the group to discuss:** When have you felt like someone was watching over you?
   - *Teacher: Take a few answers. Then tap to hear the angel.*
   - Then the angel answers: Jacob felt all alone that night. He didn’t know that God, and a whole ladder full of angels, were right there with him.
13. **The angel:** What do you want to ask me?
   1. *What do angels look like?* → The Torah doesn’t say what the angels on the ladder looked like. Artists have imagined us in many different ways. / *[Pictures: Illuminated Manuscript, Synagogue Mosaic, Blown Glass, Holographic.]* Four artists, four ideas.
   2. *Why did the angels go up first?* → Good eye! Rashi noticed that too. He said the angels who guarded Jacob in the Land of Israel went up, and new angels came down to go with him outside the Land. Like changing shifts! <sub>Rashi on Genesis 28:12</sub>
   3. *How many angels were there?* → The Talmud counts at least four: two going up and two coming down, passing each other on the ladder. <sub>Chullin 91b · the ladder</sub>
   4. *How wide was the ladder?* → The Talmud says eight thousand parasangs! A parasang is a few miles, so that’s thousands and thousands of miles wide. <sub>Chullin 91b · the ladder</sub> / Why so wide? Four angels had to fit, and the Talmud says each of us is gigantic!
   5. *Do angels watch over us today?* → Many Jewish families say a bedtime prayer that asks for four angels: Michael on your right, Gabriel on your left, Uriel in front of you, Raphael behind you, and above your head, God’s presence. <sub>Bedtime Sh’ma</sub> / Just like Jacob that night: his head on a stone, and God standing over him.
14. **The rung:** “Your face is known in heaven.”
   - **The secret:** The Sages said the angels went up to look at Jacob’s picture in heaven, then came down and found him sleeping.
15. Thanks for climbing up to see me! Rashi has more to say about why we went up first, and the Sages have lots of ideas about the ladder. *(Suggests: Rashi, The Sages, Jacob.)*

## Grades 5–7

*The fuller story, the sources, debate, and typed questions.*

1. I’m a *malach*. The word means “messenger,” and the Torah uses it for human messengers too. When Moses sends messengers to the king of Edom, the Hebrew calls them *malachim*. <sub>Numbers 20:14</sub>
2. In Jacob’s dream, “a stairway was set on the ground and its top reached to the sky, and angels of God were going up and down on it.” That was us. <sub>Genesis 28:12</sub>
3. *[Animation: Blown Glass, from the Jacob’s Dream gallery.]*
4. What were we doing? There are at least three answers, and each comes from a different teacher.
5. Rashi: changing shifts. The angels who guarded Jacob in the Land of Israel went up, and new ones came down to go with him outside the Land. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 28:12</sub>
6. The midrash: we went up to look at Jacob’s image, carved on high, and came down to find him asleep. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
7. Ramban: this is how God runs the world. “Whatever is done on earth is effected by means of the angels.” We go up to report, and come back down with orders. <sub>Ramban on Genesis 28:12</sub>
8. **The angel asks the group to discuss:** A shift change, a trip to see Jacob, or messengers reporting in. Which would you want to be true, and why?
   - *Teacher: Let students argue for their pick. Then tap to hear the angel.*
   - Then the angel answers: Each one tells Jacob something he needed that night. You’re not alone. You’re known in heaven. And God is watching over you directly.
9. The Talmud tells a sharper story. The angels who gazed at Jacob’s image wanted to endanger him. Rashi says they were jealous. So right away, God stood over Jacob to guard him. <sub>Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>
10. Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish said that if the verse didn’t say it, no one would dare to: God stood over Jacob like a parent waving over a child. Rashi explains: waving a fan, to save the child from the heat. <sub>Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>
11. **The angel asks the group to discuss:** Why would angels be jealous of a human being?
   - *Teacher: Take a few theories. Then tap to hear the angel.*
   - Then the angel answers: Here’s my guess: you can change, and we can’t. Jacob left home a trickster. Twenty years later, after a night of wrestling, he came back with a new name: Israel. <sub>Genesis 32:25–29</sub>
12. We were there at the end of the story too. On his way home, “Jacob went on his way, and angels of God encountered him.” He named the place Mahanaim, from the word for “camp.” <sub>Genesis 32:2–3</sub>

   2. *Do angels have free will?* → Ramban says we do nothing, great or small, without orders from above. <sub>Ramban on Genesis 28:12</sub> / But in the Talmud, some angels wanted to endanger Jacob, and Rashi says they were jealous. That sounds like minds of their own. You decide. <sub>Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>

## Parents

*Adult depth, with the Hebrew and the commentators.*

1. I’m a *malach*, a messenger. The Torah uses the same word for people: Moses sends *malachim* to the king of Edom. When Jacob sends *malachim* to Esau, Rashi says they were actual angels. Angel or human, a malach is defined by the errand. <sub>Numbers 20:14; Genesis 32:4; Rashi on Genesis 32:4</sub>
2. The verse about us ends with a small word: וְהִנֵּה מַלְאֲכֵי אֱלֹהִים עֹלִים וְיֹרְדִים בּוֹ  The angels go up and down *bo*: “on it,” or “on him.” <sub>Genesis 28:12</sub>
3. *[Animation: Goldwork Embroidery, from the Jacob’s Dream gallery.]*
4. Rabbi Chiya and Rabbi Yannai split on it. One read “on the ladder.” The other read “on Jacob.” The midrash doesn’t say which rabbi held which view. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
5. The one who read “on Jacob” said: your likeness is engraved on high. The midrash borrows a Greek word for it, *ikonin*, an icon. We went up and saw his image, and came down and found him asleep. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
6. The midrash compares it to a king: in the basilica they find him sitting in judgment; out in the courtyard, they find him asleep. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
7. **The angel asks the group to discuss:** The image in heaven and the man asleep on a stone. Which is the real Jacob? How do you hold both, about yourself or about your child?
   - *Teacher: Pairs first. Then tap to hear the angel.*
   - Then the angel answers: The midrash answers with a verse from Isaiah: “Israel, in whom I glory.” The pride is for the sleeper too. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
8. The Talmud tells it darker. The same angels who gazed at his image wanted to endanger him, and Rashi supplies the motive: jealousy. So God stood over Jacob to guard him. <sub>Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>
9. Reish Lakish said that were it not written in the verse, it would be impossible to say: God stood over Jacob “like a man who waves… over his son.” Rashi adds: with a fan, to save him from the heat. <sub>Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>
10. Ramban reads us as the machinery of providence. We go up to report and come down with orders. But God stands above the ladder and promises Jacob he will not be left to us: he will be God’s own portion. <sub>Ramban on Genesis 28:12</sub>
11. **The angel asks the group to discuss:** Ramban says most of the world runs through intermediaries, but Jacob receives God directly. Where do you meet God through messengers, and where, if anywhere, directly?
   - *Teacher: Give pairs a few minutes. Then tap to hear the angel.*
   - Then the angel answers: The bedtime Sh’ma asks for both: four of us, Michael, Gabriel, Uriel, and Raphael, and then, over your head, the Presence of God. <sub>Bedtime Sh’ma</sub>
12. Rambam goes further: the angels on the ladder are prophets, human beings who climb in understanding and come down to teach. <sub>Guide for the Perplexed 1:15</sub>
13. Vayeitzei opens and closes with us. Leaving home, Jacob dreams of angels going up and down. Coming home, “angels of God encountered him,” and he named the place Mahanaim. <sub>Genesis 28:12; Genesis 32:2–3</sub>
14. **The angel:** Ask the angel anything. Choose a question, or type your own. *(Typing is on: typed questions are matched to these.)*
   1. *What do angels look like?* → The Torah doesn’t say what the angels on the ladder looked like. Artists have imagined us in every way. / *[Pictures: Illuminated Manuscript, Woven Tapestry, Classical Painting, Modernist Dream.]* Four artists, four answers.
   2. *Why do the angels go up first?* → The midrash, which Rashi follows: the angels who accompanied Jacob in the Land of Israel ascend, and those who will accompany him outside the Land descend. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · his guardians; Rashi on Genesis 28:12</sub>
   3. *What does ikonin mean?* → It’s the Greek *eikon*, an image or portrait, borrowed into the Rabbis’ Hebrew: Jacob’s likeness, engraved on high. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
   4. *Do angels have wills of their own?* → Ramban says we do nothing, great or small, without orders. But the Talmud’s angels wanted to endanger Jacob, and Rashi calls it jealousy. That suggests wills of their own. <sub>Ramban on Genesis 28:12; Chullin 91b · the fan; Rashi on Chullin 91b</sub>
   5. *Who wrestled with Jacob?* → The Hebrew says only *ish*, “a man.” Rashi says the Rabbis identified him as Esau’s guardian angel. <sub>Genesis 32:25–29; Rashi on Genesis 32:25</sub>
   6. *What does Mahanaim mean?* → Jacob said, “This is God’s camp,” and named the place Mahanaim. The name comes from *machaneh*, “camp,” and its form suggests two camps. Soon after, afraid of Esau, Jacob split his own people into two camps. <sub>Genesis 32:2–3; Genesis 32:8</sub>
   7. *Where are angels in our prayers?* → In the Kedushah, the congregation repeats the angels’ words from Isaiah: “Holy, holy, holy.” / And at bedtime, many families ask for four angels to surround them. <sub>Bedtime Sh’ma</sub>
   8. *Are angels real?* → Ramban reads us as real messengers who carry out God’s decrees. Rambam reads the angels of this dream as prophets. Visit them both. <sub>Ramban on Genesis 28:12; Guide for the Perplexed 1:15</sub>
15. **The rung:** “They looked for his face in heaven and found him asleep on earth.” עֹלִים לְמַעְלָה וְרוֹאִים אִיקוֹנִין שֶׁלּוֹ, וְיֹרְדִים לְמַטָּה וּמוֹצְאִים אוֹתוֹ יָשֵׁן
   - **Ask your child:** Where do you feel closest to God?
16. For the two great readings of us, visit Rambam and Ramban. *(Suggests: Rambam, Ramban, Jacob.)*
