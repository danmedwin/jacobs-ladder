## Grades K–2

*Short lines, pictures, and moving around. The teacher reads aloud.*

1. Hello! I’m Rebekah. I’m Jacob’s mother, and Esau’s mother too.
2. When I was young, I went to the well to get water. A man was there with ten camels. <sub>Genesis 24:10–11 · Genesis 24:15</sub>
3. He asked me for a drink, so I gave him some water. Then I said, “I will also draw for your camels, until they finish drinking.” <sub>Genesis 24:17–20</sub>
4. **Everybody moves: Water for the camels!** Pull the bucket up from the well… pull, pull, pull! Pour it out. Again! Ten thirsty camels!
5. That man had come a long way to find a wife for Isaac. My family asked me, “Will you go with this man?” And I said, “I will.” <sub>Genesis 24:14 · Genesis 24:58</sub>
6. So I rode a camel all the way to my new home, and I married Isaac. <sub>Genesis 24:61 · Genesis 24:67</sub>
7. Later, I had twin boys: Esau and Jacob! Esau loved to be outside. Jacob liked to stay home, near the tents. <sub>Genesis 25:24–26 · Genesis 25:27</sub>
8. When the boys grew up, I made a plan so that Jacob would get his father’s special blessing. Jacob tricked his father, and the trick made Esau very, very angry. <sub>Genesis 27:5–17 · Genesis 27:41</sub>
9. I was scared for Jacob. So I told him, “Go far away to my brother Laban. Stay there until Esau isn’t angry anymore.” <sub>Genesis 27:42–45</sub>
10. **Everybody moves: Pack a bag!** Let’s help Jacob pack. Put in some bread… a warm blanket… and a big hug from Mom. Zip it up!
11. **Rebekah asks:** How do you think I felt when Jacob left?
   - Button **“Sad”** → Yes. It’s hard to say goodbye to someone you love.
   - Button **“Worried”** → Yes. He was going so far away, all by himself.
   - Button **“Hopeful”** → Yes! I hoped he would be safe, and that one day he would come home.
   - *Teacher: Let everyone answer out loud. Then choose what most of the group said.*
12. Jacob was gone for a long, long time. But he wasn’t alone. On his way, God told him, “I am with you.” <sub>Genesis 28:15</sub>
13. **Rebekah asks the group to discuss:** Have you ever said goodbye to someone you love? What helped?
   - *Teacher: Take a few answers out loud. Then tap to hear Rebekah.*
   - Then Rebekah answers: A hug helps. So do the words you say. I wanted Jacob to know that I sent him away because I love him.
14. **Rebekah:** What would you like to ask me?
   1. *Did you ride a camel?* → I did! When I first saw Isaac, walking in a field, I got down off my camel to meet him. <sub>Genesis 24:61 · Genesis 24:64–65</sub>
   2. *Why did you make that plan?* → Before my boys were born, God told me that the older one would serve the younger one. I thought I was helping that happen. <sub>Genesis 25:23</sub> / But the trick hurt people I loved. What do you think I should have done?
   3. *Were you brave?* → I think so! I said “I will” and went far away to a new home, where I didn’t know anyone. <sub>Genesis 24:58</sub>
   4. *What was Jacob like?* → Quiet. He liked to stay home near the tents. His brother Esau loved being outside. <sub>Genesis 25:27</sub>
15. **The rung:** “I sent you away because I love you.”
   - **The motion:** Give yourself a hug. Then wave goodbye.
16. Thank you for visiting me! Who do you want to meet next? *(Suggests: Jacob, Esau, The stone.)*

## Grades 3–4

*Short lines, choices, and puzzles. Students read along.*

1. I’m Rebekah, Jacob’s mother. I’m the one who sent him away, and I’ll tell you why. But my story starts at a well.
2. A stranger came to our town with ten camels and asked me for a drink. I gave him water, and then I said, “I will also draw for your camels, until they finish drinking.” <sub>Genesis 24:10 · Genesis 24:17–20</sub>
3. He turned out to be the servant of Abraham, my grandfather’s brother. He had come to find a wife for Abraham’s son, Isaac. <sub>Genesis 24:14 · Genesis 24:15</sub>
4. My family asked me, “Will you go with this man?” And I said, “I will.” <sub>Genesis 24:58</sub>
5. **Rebekah asks:** Would you leave home to go somewhere you’d never been, to live with people you’d never met?
   - Button **“Yes, what an adventure!”** → That’s how I felt. My family wanted me to wait about ten days. When they asked me, I said I would go right away. <sub>Genesis 24:55 · Genesis 24:58</sub>
   - Button **“No way!”** → It was a big step. I had never even met Isaac. But when they asked me, I said yes.
   - Button **“Only if someone came with me”** → Someone did! My nurse came with me. Her name was Deborah. Remember her: she comes back at the end of my story. <sub>Genesis 24:59 · Genesis 35:8</sub>
   - *Teacher: Hear a few answers. Then choose one.*
6. I married Isaac. For years we had no children. Then I was expecting twins, and they struggled inside me so much that I said, “If so, why do I exist?” <sub>Genesis 25:21 · Genesis 25:22</sub>
7. I went to ask God. God told me that two nations were inside me, and “the older shall serve the younger.” <sub>Genesis 25:23</sub>
8. My boys grew up very different. And the Torah says it plainly: “Isaac favored Esau because he had a taste for game; but Rebekah favored Jacob.” <sub>Genesis 25:28</sub>
9. When Isaac was old and blind, I heard him tell Esau to hunt and cook for him, so that he could bless him. <sub>Genesis 27:1–5</sub>
10. I told Jacob to bring me two young goats. I cooked them the way Isaac liked, dressed Jacob in Esau’s best clothes, and covered his arms and neck with the goatskins. <sub>Genesis 27:9 · Genesis 27:14–16</sub>
11. Jacob was afraid he’d be caught and get a curse instead of a blessing. I said, “Your curse, my son, be upon me!” <sub>Genesis 27:12 · Genesis 27:13</sub>
12. **Rebekah asks the group to discuss:** Was I right to help Jacob trick his father?
   - *Teacher: Let students argue both sides. Then tap to hear Rebekah.*
   - Then Rebekah answers: I believed what God told me: the older would serve the younger. I thought I was helping it come true. <sub>Genesis 25:23</sub> / But look what happened. Esau was heartbroken, and I had to send my son away. What do you think now?
13. When I heard that Esau wanted to hurt Jacob, I told Jacob, “Flee at once to Haran, to my brother Laban.” <sub>Genesis 27:42 · Genesis 27:43</sub>
14. I told him to stay there awhile. In Hebrew I said *yamim achadim*, “a few days.” And I promised, “Then I will fetch you from there.” <sub>Genesis 27:44 · Genesis 27:45</sub>
15. But I didn’t tell Isaac about Esau’s threat. I told him I was worried that Jacob would marry one of the local women. <sub>Genesis 27:46</sub>
16. **Rebekah asks:** Why do you think I didn’t tell Isaac the real reason?
   - Button **“So Isaac wouldn’t be upset”** → Maybe. He was old and blind. Hearing that one son wanted to hurt the other might have been too much.
   - Button **“To keep Esau out of trouble”** → Maybe. Esau is my son too.
   - Button **“So Isaac would bless Jacob again”** → It worked. Isaac called Jacob in and blessed him again, and this time he knew exactly who Jacob was. <sub>Genesis 28:1–4</sub>
   - *Teacher: Let students explain their choice before you tap it.*
17. It wasn’t a few days. Jacob stayed away for twenty years. <sub>Genesis 31:41</sub>
18. **Rebekah:** What do you want to ask me?
   1. *Did you ever see Jacob again?* → The Torah never tells us. It doesn’t even tell us when I died. / But Rashi noticed something. When Jacob was on his way home, my old nurse Deborah was with him. Why? Rashi says I kept my promise: I sent Deborah to tell Jacob it was time to come home. <sub>Genesis 35:8 · Rashi on Genesis 35:8</sub> / Deborah died on the way, near Beit El. They buried her under an oak and named it Allon-bacuth, the Oak of Weeping. Rashi says that there, Jacob also learned that I had died. <sub>Genesis 35:8 · Rashi on Genesis 35:8</sub>
   2. *Why did you love Jacob more?* → The Torah doesn’t say why. It tells us that Isaac favored Esau because of the game Esau hunted, and that I favored Jacob. <sub>Genesis 25:28</sub> / Maybe it was because of what God told me before my boys were born. <sub>Genesis 25:23</sub>
   3. *Was it hard to leave home?* → My family wanted me to wait about ten days. When they asked me, I said I would go right away. <sub>Genesis 24:55 · Genesis 24:58</sub>
   4. *What was Jacob like?* → The Torah says Jacob was “a mild man who stayed in camp,” and Esau was “a skillful hunter, a man of the outdoors.” <sub>Genesis 25:27</sub>
19. **The rung:** “Sometimes loving someone means letting them go.”
   - **The secret:** In Hebrew, I told Jacob to stay away “a few days.” It became twenty years.
20. Thank you for visiting. Isaac can tell you about the blessing, and Esau has his own side of the story. *(Suggests: Isaac, Esau, Jacob.)*
