# Isaac’s visit: the script

A readable copy of what the tool says, for review. It is generated from `voices/data/isaac.js`, so mark changes here and they will be carried into the data.

Small gray references are the source cards a teacher can open during the visit. Numbered lines are the steps the teacher moves through with Next.

A character speaks only from their own life and what they could know. Anything else is marked **Teacher says:** and is read by the teacher in their own voice; it appears on screen under the character’s line. *(imagined)* marks a line that fits the story and its world but that no text says; the screen labels it “Imagined.”

## Grades 3–4

*Short lines, choices, and puzzles. Students read along.*

1. I’m Isaac, father of Jacob and Esau. When all this happened, I was old, and my eyes were too dim to see. <sub>Genesis 27:1</sub>
2. I called my older son, Esau. I asked him to hunt some game and cook me the dish I love, so that I could give him my innermost blessing before I died. <sub>Genesis 27:1–4</sub>
3. Soon, someone came in and said, “Father.” I asked, “Which of my sons are you?” <sub>Genesis 27:18</sub>
4. He said, “I am Esau, your first-born.” But he had come back so fast! I asked, “How did you succeed so quickly, my son?” <sub>Genesis 27:19; Genesis 27:20</sub>
5. So I said, “Come closer that I may feel you, my son.” <sub>Genesis 27:21</sub>
6. **Everybody moves: Guess the voice!** Everyone close your eyes. The teacher taps one person, who says “Shalom!” Can you guess who it was, just from the voice?
7. His arms felt hairy, like Esau’s. But his voice… I said, “The voice is the voice of Jacob, yet the hands are the hands of Esau.” <sub>Genesis 27:22</sub>
8. I said, “Come close and kiss me, my son.” When he kissed me, I smelled his clothes. They smelled like the fields, like Esau. So I blessed him. <sub>Genesis 27:26–29</sub>
9. Right after Jacob left, Esau came in with his food. I asked, “Who are you?” He said, “I am your son, Esau, your first-born!” <sub>Genesis 27:30–32</sub>
10. I was seized with very violent trembling. I had blessed someone else! But I said, “Now he must remain blessed!” <sub>Genesis 27:33</sub>
11. **Isaac asks:** Why do you think I didn’t take the blessing back?
   - Button **“A blessing can’t be taken back”** → That’s how it felt to me. Once words are spoken, they’re out in the world.
   - Button **“Maybe God wanted it that way”** → Maybe. Before the twins were born, God told Rebekah that the older would serve the younger. <sub>Genesis 25:23</sub>
   - Button **“He still loved Jacob”** → I did. Jacob tricked me, but he was still my son.
   - *Teacher: Let students explain their choice before you tap it.*
12. Esau begged me, “Bless me too, Father!” So I gave him a blessing too. <sub>Genesis 27:38; Genesis 27:39–40</sub>
13. Later, Rebekah told me she didn’t want Jacob marrying one of the local women. So I called Jacob in, and I blessed him again. <sub>Genesis 27:46; Genesis 28:1</sub>
14. This time, I knew exactly who he was. I said, “May you and your offspring be granted the blessing of Abraham.” <sub>Genesis 28:3–4</sub>
15. **Isaac asks the group to discuss:** Jacob already had my blessing. Why would I bless him again?
   - *Teacher: Take a few answers. Then tap to hear Isaac.*
   - Then Isaac answers: The first blessing was for someone pretending to be Esau. This one was for Jacob, as himself. / And a blessing is a way to say goodbye. He was leaving home, and I wanted him to carry something from me.
16. Later, in Jacob’s dream, God said, “I am the Eternal, the God of your father Abraham and the God of Isaac.” That’s me! Jacob heard my name in his dream. <sub>Genesis 28:13</sub>
17. **Isaac:** What do you want to ask me?
   1. *Were you angry at Jacob?* → I told Esau, “Your brother came with guile and took away your blessing.” Guile means sneaky tricks. <sub>Genesis 27:35</sub> / But I didn’t take the blessing back. And later, I blessed Jacob again. <sub>Genesis 28:1–4</sub>
   2. *What’s your favorite food?* → Game! I had a taste for the wild animals Esau hunted. <sub>Genesis 25:28</sub> / But Rebekah cooked two young goats just the way I liked, and I couldn’t tell the difference! <sub>Genesis 27:9; Genesis 27:14</sub>
   3. *What does a blessing do?* → A blessing is a wish for someone’s future, said out loud. In my family, a father’s blessing was a very big deal. **Teacher says:** Jewish parents still bless their children on Friday night, with words that go back to Jacob blessing his grandsons. For daughters, parents say, “May God make you like Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, and Leah.” <sub>Genesis 48:20</sub> / Rebekah? That’s my Rebekah!
   4. *Did you ever see Jacob again?* → Yes! Many years later, Jacob came home to me at Mamre, in Hebron. <sub>Genesis 35:27</sub> **Teacher says:** When Isaac died, both of his sons, Esau and Jacob, buried him together. <sub>Genesis 35:29</sub>
   5. *How old were you?* → I was sixty when the twins were born, and I lived to be a hundred and eighty! <sub>Genesis 25:26; Genesis 35:28</sub>
18. **The rung:** “I gave you the blessing of Abraham. Take it with you.”
   - **The secret:** I blessed Jacob twice: once when he tricked me, and once when he left home.
19. Thank you for visiting an old man. Rebekah can tell you why she helped Jacob, and Esau can tell you how it felt to lose the blessing. *(Suggests: Rebekah, Esau, Jacob.)*

## Grades 5–7

*The fuller story, the sources, debate, and typed questions.*

1. I’m Isaac, Yitzchak in Hebrew. My name means “he will laugh.” There wasn’t much laughing in what I’m about to tell you.
2. When I was old and my eyes were too dim to see, I called my older son and told him, “Take your gear, your quiver and bow, and go out into the open and hunt me some game.” Then I would give him my innermost blessing. <sub>Genesis 27:1–4</sub>
3. Someone came in and said, “Father.” I asked, “Yes, which of my sons are you?” He said, “I am Esau, your first-born.” <sub>Genesis 27:18; Genesis 27:19</sub>
4. He was back too soon. I asked, “How did you succeed so quickly, my son?” He said, “Because the Eternal your God granted me good fortune.” <sub>Genesis 27:20</sub>
5. I said, “Come closer that I may feel you, my son.” And then: “The voice is the voice of Jacob, yet the hands are the hands of Esau.” <sub>Genesis 27:21; Genesis 27:22</sub>
6. **Isaac asks:** My ears said Jacob. My hands said Esau. Why did I bless him anyway?
   - Button **“He trusted his hands”** → I did. His hands were hairy like Esau’s, and so I blessed him. **Teacher says:** That’s what the Torah says: “He did not recognize him, because his hands were hairy like those of his brother Esau; and so he blessed him.” <sub>Genesis 27:23</sub>
   - Button **“He wanted to believe it”** → Maybe. I asked one more time, “Are you really my son Esau?” and he said, “I am.” <sub>Genesis 27:24</sub>
   - Button **“He knew, and did it anyway”** → I won’t tell you. What makes you suspect it? **Teacher says:** The Torah never says Isaac knew, but some readers think so.
   - *Teacher: Ask students to point to the words in the story that support their choice.*
7. I asked him to come close and kiss me. When he did, I smelled his clothes and said, “Ah, the smell of my son is like the smell of the fields that God has blessed.” <sub>Genesis 27:26–27</sub>
8. And I blessed him: “May God give you Of the dew of heaven and the fat of the earth… Be master over your brothers.” <sub>Genesis 27:28–29</sub>
9. Right after Jacob left, Esau came in with his dish. I asked, “Who are you?” He said, “I am your son, Esau, your first-born!” <sub>Genesis 27:30–32</sub>
10. I was seized with very violent trembling. I asked who had brought me the meal, and then I said, “I blessed him; now he must remain blessed!” <sub>Genesis 27:33</sub>
11. **Isaac asks the group to discuss:** Why couldn’t I take it back? Is a blessing a promise, a gift, or something else?
   - *Teacher: Split the group: “He should have taken it back” against “He was right to keep it.” Then tap to hear Isaac.*
   - Then Isaac answers: Words spoken over a child are hard to take back. Maybe that’s the point. **Teacher says:** Rashi says Isaac confirmed it on purpose, so that no one could say Jacob got the blessing only by tricking him. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 27:33 · the blessing stands</sub>
12. I told Esau, “Your brother came with guile and took away your blessing.” He begged for a blessing of his own. I asked, “What, then, can I still do for you, my son?” And I found words for him too. <sub>Genesis 27:35; Genesis 27:37; Genesis 27:38–40</sub>
13. Later, I sent for Jacob and blessed him, by his own name this time: “May El Shaddai bless you… May you and your offspring be granted the blessing of Abraham.” <sub>Genesis 28:1; Genesis 28:3–4</sub>
14. **Isaac asks:** What’s different about the second blessing?
   - Button **“It was honest”** → Yes. The first was for someone pretending to be Esau. The second was for Jacob.
   - Button **“It was a different blessing”** → It was. The first was about dew, grain, wine, and power over his brothers. The second passed on the blessing of Abraham: children, and the land God promised. <sub>Genesis 27:28–29; Genesis 28:3–4</sub>
   - Button **“It was a goodbye”** → It was. Right after it, I sent Jacob off. <sub>Genesis 28:5</sub>
   - *Teacher: If there’s time, open both blessings on the source cards and compare them.*
15. Later, in Jacob’s dream, God said, “I am the Eternal, the God of your father Abraham and the God of Isaac.” Jacob heard my name at Beit El. <sub>Genesis 28:13</sub>
16. **Isaac:** Ask me anything. Choose a question, or type your own. *(Typing is on: typed questions are matched to these.)*
   1. *Did you know it was Jacob?* → I asked twice: “whether you are really my son Esau or not,” and then, “Are you really my son Esau?” He said, “I am.” The rest I leave to you. <sub>Genesis 27:21; Genesis 27:24</sub>
   2. *Were you angry at Jacob?* → I told Esau, “Your brother came with guile and took away your blessing.” But I didn’t take the blessing back, and later I blessed Jacob again. <sub>Genesis 27:35; Genesis 28:1–4</sub>
   3. *Why were your eyes dim?* → I was old, and they grew dim. I can’t tell you more than that. <sub>Genesis 27:1</sub> **Teacher says:** Rashi gives three reasons. One: the smoke of the incense Esau’s wives burned to idols. Two: when Isaac lay bound on the altar and his father was about to sacrifice him, the angels wept, and their tears fell into his eyes. Three: so that Jacob could receive the blessing. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 27:1</sub>
   4. *Why did you love Esau more?* → I had a taste for the game he hunted. Was that the whole reason? What do you think? <sub>Genesis 25:28</sub>
   5. *What did you give Esau?* → “See, your abode shall enjoy the fat of the earth And the dew of heaven above. Yet by your sword you shall live, And you shall serve your brother; But when you grow restive, You shall break his yoke from your neck.” <sub>Genesis 27:39–40</sub>
   6. *Did you see Jacob again?* → Yes. Jacob came home to me at Mamre. <sub>Genesis 35:27</sub> **Teacher says:** And when Isaac died, in ripe old age, “he was buried by his sons Esau and Jacob.” <sub>Genesis 35:29</sub>
   7. *Why does God say “the God of Isaac”?* → I was still alive when God said it. <sub>Genesis 28:13</sub> **Teacher says:** Rashi noticed that God doesn’t usually attach the divine name to someone still alive. He says God made an exception for Isaac, because he was blind and stuck at home, as if his days of doing wrong were over. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 28:13 · the God of Isaac</sub>
   8. *How old were you?* → Sixty when the twins were born, and a hundred and eighty when I died. <sub>Genesis 25:26; Genesis 35:28</sub>
   9. *What does a blessing do?* → A blessing puts a hope for someone’s future into words, out loud, where they can hear it. **Teacher says:** Jewish parents still bless their children on Friday night, with words from Isaac’s son Jacob. <sub>Genesis 48:20</sub>
17. **The rung:** “The second blessing was the one he didn’t have to steal.”
   - **Ask your parents:** What blessing would you give me as I grow up?
18. Go hear Rebekah, who planned it all, and Esau, who lost the blessing. *(Suggests: Rebekah, Esau, Jacob.)*

## Parents

*Adult depth, with the Hebrew and the commentators.*

1. I’m Isaac. You may remember me bound on an altar by my own father. This time I am the father: old, blind, and holding a blessing.
   - **Teacher says:** Rashi gives three reasons Isaac’s eyes grew dim. The one people remember: when he lay bound on the altar, the heavens opened, the angels wept, and their tears fell into his eyes. The other two: the smoke of his daughters-in-law’s idols, or so that Jacob could receive the blessing. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 27:1</sub>
2. **Isaac asks the group to discuss:** Tears from the altar, smoke in my own house, or a plan I could not see. Which explanation speaks to you, and why?
   - *Teacher: Pairs first. Then tap to hear Isaac.*
   - Then Isaac answers: Maybe all three are true at once. A father can carry a wound from his own childhood, miss what is happening in his own house, and still be part of a larger story he can’t see.
3. I asked Esau to hunt and prepare my favorite dish, “so that I may give you my innermost blessing before I die.” My own words were בַּעֲבוּר תְּבָרֶכְךָ נַפְשִׁי  “so that my soul may bless you.” <sub>Genesis 27:4</sub>
4. Then Jacob came in, dressed as his brother. I felt his hands and said, הַקֹּל קוֹל יַעֲקֹב וְהַיָּדַיִם יְדֵי עֵשָׂו  “The voice is the voice of Jacob, yet the hands are the hands of Esau.” <sub>Genesis 27:21; Genesis 27:22</sub>
5. **Isaac asks the group to discuss:** I heard Jacob’s voice and blessed him anyway. Did I know? Have you ever chosen not to know something about your child?
   - *Teacher: This one is for pairs. Then tap to hear Isaac.*
   - Then Isaac answers: His hands were hairy like Esau’s, and I blessed him. What I suspected, and what I chose not to ask, I have kept to myself. **Teacher says:** The Torah says, “He did not recognize him, because his hands were hairy like those of his brother Esau.” It doesn’t say what Isaac suspected, or what he chose not to ask. <sub>Genesis 27:23</sub>
6. When Esau came in, I was seized with very violent trembling. <sub>Genesis 27:33</sub>
   - **Teacher says:** Rashi quotes the Aramaic translation, which reads the trembling as bewilderment. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 27:33 · the trembling</sub>
7. And then I said, גַּם־בָּרוּךְ יִהְיֶה  “now he must remain blessed!” <sub>Genesis 27:33</sub>
   - **Teacher says:** Rashi says Isaac confirmed it deliberately, so that no one could say Jacob got the blessing only through deceit. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 27:33 · the blessing stands</sub>
8. **Isaac asks the group to discuss:** Why would a father ratify a blessing obtained by deception? Wisdom, weakness, or faith?
   - *Teacher: Let people disagree. Then tap to hear Isaac.*
   - Then Isaac answers: Maybe I understood that the blessing had found the son who would carry it. Maybe I simply couldn’t take my own words back. I’ll let you decide.
9. Esau cried out, and I asked him the saddest question a father can ask: “What, then, can I still do for you, my son?” Then I blessed him too. <sub>Genesis 27:37; Genesis 27:39–40</sub>
10. When Jacob left, I called him in and blessed him again, by his own name, with the blessing of Abraham: וְיִתֶּן־לְךָ אֶת־בִּרְכַּת אַבְרָהָם לְךָ וּלְזַרְעֲךָ אִתָּךְ  “May you and your offspring be granted the blessing of Abraham.” <sub>Genesis 28:1; Genesis 28:3–4</sub>
11. **Isaac asks the group to discuss:** What blessing did your parents give you, spoken or unspoken? What blessing do you want to give your child before they leave home?
   - *Teacher: Pairs only, with no need to share with the room. Then tap to hear Isaac.*
   - Then Isaac answers: I waited until my son was leaving home. You don’t have to. **Teacher says:** Later this morning, you’ll have a chance to say it out loud.
12. In Jacob’s dream, God said, “I am the Eternal, the God of your father Abraham and the God of Isaac.” I was still living when God said it. <sub>Genesis 28:13</sub>
   - **Teacher says:** Rashi notes that God doesn’t usually attach the divine name to someone still living. He says an exception was made for Isaac: blind and confined to the house, he was as good as dead, past the reach of temptation. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 28:13 · the God of Isaac</sub>
13. **Isaac:** Ask me anything. Choose a question, or type your own. *(Typing is on: typed questions are matched to these.)*
   1. *Did you know it was Jacob?* → I asked twice: “whether you are really my son Esau or not,” and then, “Are you really my son Esau?” He said, “I am.” <sub>Genesis 27:21; Genesis 27:24</sub> / The rest I leave to you.
   2. *Were you angry at Rebekah?* → That is between Rebekah and me. **Teacher says:** The Torah never says. It never shows Isaac and Rebekah speaking about the blessing at all.
   3. *Why did you love Esau more?* → I had a taste for the game he hunted. <sub>Genesis 25:28</sub> **Teacher says:** The Hebrew says, literally, because game was in his mouth. Rashi also brings a midrash: there was “hunting in Esau’s mouth,” words he used “to entrap and deceive” his father. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 25:28</sub>
   4. *Why were your eyes dim?* → I was old, and they grew dim. I can’t tell you more than that. <sub>Genesis 27:1</sub> **Teacher says:** Rashi’s three reasons: the smoke of the incense Esau’s wives burned to idols; the angels’ tears at the altar; or so that Jacob could receive the blessing. <sub>Rashi on Genesis 27:1</sub>
   5. *What did you give Esau?* → “See, your abode shall enjoy the fat of the earth And the dew of heaven above. Yet by your sword you shall live, And you shall serve your brother; But when you grow restive, You shall break his yoke from your neck.” <sub>Genesis 27:39–40</sub>
   6. *Did you see Jacob again?* → Yes. Jacob came home to me at Mamre. <sub>Genesis 35:27</sub> **Teacher says:** When Isaac died, “he was buried by his sons Esau and Jacob.” <sub>Genesis 35:29</sub>
   7. *Where does the Friday night blessing come from?* → In my family, a blessing was handed from parent to child. I’m glad to hear it still is. **Teacher says:** It comes from Isaac’s son. When Jacob blessed his grandsons, he said, “By you shall Israel invoke blessings, saying: God make you like Ephraim and Manasseh.” For daughters, parents name the four mothers: Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, and Leah. <sub>Genesis 48:20</sub>
14. **The rung:** “May God give you the blessing of Abraham.” וְיִתֶּן־לְךָ אֶת־בִּרְכַּת אַבְרָהָם
   - **Ask your child:** What blessing would you want from me?
15. You’ve heard a father. Now hear the mother who made the plan, and the son who lost the blessing. *(Suggests: Rebekah, Esau, Ramban.)*
