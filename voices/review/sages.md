## Grades 3–4

*Short lines, choices, and puzzles. Students read along.*

1. Shalom! We’re the Sages, rabbis who lived in the Land of Israel long ago. Our teachings were collected about fifteen hundred years ago.
2. We wrote midrash. Midrash comes from a Hebrew word that means “to search.” We searched every word of the Torah for its meaning.
3. And we loved giving more than one answer. When we had another idea, we said *davar acher*, “another thing.” Say it with us: *Davar acher!*
4. Jacob dreamed of a ladder set on the ground with its top reaching the sky. But what was that ladder? We had lots of answers. <sub>Genesis 28:12</sub>
5. **The Sages ask:** What do you think the ladder stood for?
   - Button **“A way to reach God”** → *Davar acher!* Now listen to two of our answers.
   - Button **“A holy place”** → One of us thought so too! Listen to Bar Kappara.
   - Button **“A mountain”** → You’re thinking like the Rabbis! First, listen to Bar Kappara.
   - *Teacher: Hear a few ideas. Then choose one.*
6. Bar Kappara taught: every dream has a meaning. The ladder is the ramp that led up to the altar in the Temple, and the angels are the High Priests, going up and down the ramp. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Bar Kappara</sub>
7. *Davar acher!* The Rabbis said the ladder is Mount Sinai. Its bottom was on the ground, where the people stood at the foot of the mountain. Its top reached the sky, like the mountain burning with fire up to the heavens. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai</sub>
8. And the angels going up and down? That’s Moses and Aaron! Moses went up the mountain to God, and came back down to the people. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai</sub>
9. We even found a secret code. We said the letters of *sulam*, “ladder,” equal the letters of *Sinai*. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai</sub>
10. **Count it: Count it!** Every Hebrew letter is also a number. Add up each word.
   - סֻלָּם (sulam · ladder): ס 60 + ל 30 + ם 40 = ?
   - סִינַי (Sinai): ס 60 + י 10 + נ 50 + י 10 = ?
11. **The Sages ask the group to discuss:** What did you get?
   - *Teacher: Give the group a minute to add. Then tap to hear the Sages.*
   - Then the Sages answer: Both come to 130! A later teacher, the Ba’al HaTurim, spelled it out: *sulam* equals *Sinai*. <sub>Ba’al HaTurim on Genesis 28:12 · ladder and Sinai</sub>
12. **The Sages ask the group to discuss:** We could have picked one answer and thrown out the rest. Why do you think we kept them all?
   - *Teacher: Take a few answers. Then tap to hear the Sages.*
   - Then the Sages answer: Because one verse of Torah is big enough to hold more than one good answer. / And because arguing respectfully is one of the best ways to learn. We call it an argument for the sake of heaven.
13. **The Sages:** What do you want to ask us?
   1. *Which answer is right?* → We never decided! In the midrash, our answers sit side by side. Which one do you like best?
   2. *Did Jacob climb the ladder?* → Rabbi Meir taught that God invited Jacob to climb. But Jacob had just watched angels go up and come back down, and he was afraid he would come down too. <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · Rabbi Meir</sub> / God promised him: if you go up, you will never come down. But Jacob didn’t believe it, and he didn’t climb. <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · Rabbi Meir</sub> / What would you have done?
   3. *Can people be angels?* → In Hebrew, *malach* means “messenger.” We taught that prophets are called *malachim*, because they bring God’s message. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai</sub> / Who has been a messenger of something good for you?
   4. *Why did the angels go up first?* → We had an answer for that too. The angels who went with Jacob in the Land of Israel went up, and the angels who would go with him outside the Land came down. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · his guardians</sub> / Rashi learned that from us!
   5. *Is there a ladder today?* → The Ba’al HaTurim counted letters, too. He spelled *sulam* with a vav, סולם, and got 136. That’s the same as *kol*, קול, “voice.” <sub>Ba’al HaTurim on Genesis 28:12 · ladder and voice</sub> / He said that when good people pray, their voices make a ladder for the angels to climb. When you pray with all your heart, every rung is there.
14. **The rung:** “The ladder could be many things.”
   - **The secret:** Sulam (ladder) and Sinai add up to the same number: 130!
15. *Davar acher*: another visit! Rashi learned from us, and the angel has a story about Jacob’s picture in heaven. *(Suggests: Rashi, An angel, Jacob.)*

## Grades 5–7

*The fuller story, the sources, debate, and typed questions.*

1. We’re the Sages of the midrash: rabbis of the Land of Israel whose teachings were gathered in B’reishit Rabbah, about fifteen hundred years ago.
2. For us, a dream always means something. Bar Kappara put it this way: “There is no dream that does not have an interpretation.” <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Bar Kappara</sub>
3. *[Animation: Synagogue Mosaic, from the Jacob’s Dream gallery.]*
4. **The Sages ask:** So what is the ladder?
   - Button **“The Temple”** → That’s Bar Kappara’s reading: the ladder is the ramp up to the altar, and the angels are the High Priests going up and down it. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Bar Kappara</sub>
   - Button **“Mount Sinai”** → That’s the Rabbis’ reading: the ladder is Sinai, and the angels are Moses and Aaron. Moses went up to God and came back down to the people. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai</sub>
   - Button **“Jacob himself”** → One of us read it that way: the angels went up and down “on him.” His image was engraved on high, and they came down to find him asleep. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
   - *Teacher: Whatever the group picks, the Sages explain the others next.*
5. Here are all three, side by side. Bar Kappara: the ramp to the altar, with the High Priests going up and down. The Rabbis: Mount Sinai, with Moses and Aaron. And one of us: the angels going up and down on Jacob himself. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Bar Kappara; B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai; B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob</sub>
6. We even found a code. About the ladder and Sinai we said, “The letters of this equals the letters of that.” <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai</sub>
7. **Count it: Count it!** Every Hebrew letter is also a number. Add up each word.
   - סֻלָּם (sulam · ladder): ס 60 + ל 30 + ם 40 = ?
   - סִינַי (Sinai): ס 60 + י 10 + נ 50 + י 10 = ?
8. **The Sages ask the group to discuss:** What did you get?
   - *Teacher: Give the group a minute to add. Then tap to hear the Sages.*
   - Then the Sages answer: Both come to 130. A later teacher, the Ba’al HaTurim, spelled it out: *sulam* equals *Sinai*. <sub>Ba’al HaTurim on Genesis 28:12 · ladder and Sinai</sub>
9. We never chose one answer. In the midrash, a new reading is often introduced with *davar acher*, “another matter,” and all of them stay on the page.
10. Why did the sun set early that night? We said God darkened it to speak with Jacob privately, like a king who tells his servants, “Extinguish the lamps… as I wish to speak with my friend privately.” <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:10</sub>
11. Rabbi Meir taught that God showed Jacob the guardian angels of four empires, Babylon, Media, Greece, and Edom, each climbing the ladder and coming back down. Then God said to Jacob, “You, too, will ascend.” <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · Rabbi Meir</sub>
12. Jacob was afraid: if they all came down, so would he. God promised that if he went up, he would never come down. And Rabbi Meir says, “He did not believe, and he did not ascend.” <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · Rabbi Meir</sub>
13. **The Sages ask the group to discuss:** Would you have climbed?
   - *Teacher: Split the room into climbers and stayers. Each side gets a minute to make its case. Then tap to hear the Sages.*
   - Then the Sages answer: Rabbi Meir was hard on Jacob. But notice what Jacob feared. Not the climbing. The falling. / Is that a lack of faith, or just being human?
14. **The Sages:** Ask us anything. Choose a question, or type your own. *(Typing is on: typed questions are matched to these.)*
   1. *Which answer is right?* → We never decided. In the midrash, our answers sit side by side. Which one do you like best, and why?
   2. *What happened to the four empires?* → Another of us, Rabbi Shmuel bar Nachman, counted their rungs: Babylon climbed seventy, Media fifty-two, Greece a hundred and eighty. Edom kept climbing, and Jacob couldn’t tell how far. <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · four kingdoms</sub> / God told Jacob: even if it nests among the stars, I will bring it down. <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · four kingdoms</sub>
   3. *Why is God called “the Place”?* → The Torah says Jacob came upon “the place.” One of God’s names is HaMakom, the Place, because God is the place of the world, and the world is not God’s place. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:9</sub>
   4. *Did Jacob pray there?* → The Talmud says Jacob instituted the evening prayer: “he encountered the place” means he prayed. <sub>Berakhot 26b</sub>
   5. *Can people be angels?* → We taught that prophets are called *malachim*, because they carry God’s message. That’s how the angels on the ladder can be Moses and Aaron. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai</sub>
   6. *Is there a ladder today?* → The Ba’al HaTurim counted again. Spelled with a vav, סולם comes to 136, the same as קול, “voice.” He said the voice of the prayer of good people is a ladder for the angels to climb. <sub>Ba’al HaTurim on Genesis 28:12 · ladder and voice</sub>
   7. *Who were the Sages?* → Rabbis of the Land of Israel in the centuries after the Temple fell: Bar Kappara, Rabbi Meir, Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish, and many more. Our teachings were collected in books of midrash like B’reishit Rabbah.
15. **The rung:** “Would you have climbed?”
   - **Ask your parents:** Would you have climbed? Why or why not?
16. *Davar acher*: another visit. Rashi built on our answers, and Rambam has a reading all his own. *(Suggests: Rashi, Rambam, Jacob.)*

## Parents

*Adult depth, with the Hebrew and the commentators.*

1. We are the Sages of B’reishit Rabbah and Vayikra Rabbah. Our way of reading is plural on purpose.
2. The Hebrew says Jacob came upon *hamakom*, “the place.” We heard it as a name of God, and said, הֱוֵי הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא מְקוֹמוֹ שֶׁל עוֹלָם וְאֵין עוֹלָמוֹ מְקוֹמוֹ  “the Holy One blessed be He is the place of the world, and His world is not His place.” <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:9</sub>
3. Why did the sun set early? So that God could speak with Jacob alone, like a king who says, “Extinguish the lamps… as I wish to speak with my friend privately.” And the hours of light taken when Jacob left were given back when he came home: “The sun rose upon him.” <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:10; Genesis 32:32</sub>
4. *[Animation: Modernist Dream, from the Jacob’s Dream gallery.]*
5. Then we read the dream many ways. Here are four. Bar Kappara: “There is no dream that does not have an interpretation,” and the ladder is the ramp to the altar. The Rabbis: it is Sinai, with Moses and Aaron going up and down. One of us: the angels went up and down on Jacob himself. And another matter: the angels of the Land hand him to the angels who will go with him abroad. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Bar Kappara; B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai; B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · on Jacob; B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · his guardians</sub>
6. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi read the whole passage as exile. “Jacob departed” recalls Jeremiah’s “let them go.” The stones of the place recall Lamentations: “The sacred stones are spilled.” The ladder is Nebuchadnezzar’s idol, and the angels are Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, who would not bow. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:13</sub>
7. **The Sages ask the group to discuss:** Temple, Sinai, Jacob’s own image, exile. Why would a community keep contradictory readings of one dream side by side, in one book?
   - *Teacher: Pairs first, then the room. Then tap to hear the Sages.*
   - Then the Sages answer: Because a sacred text has to hold a people’s whole life: worship, revelation, the self, and catastrophe. We didn’t choose. We compiled.
8. In Vayikra Rabbah, Rabbi Shmuel bar Nachman counts the rungs the empires climbed: Babylon seventy, Media fifty-two, Greece a hundred and eighty. Edom kept climbing, and Jacob couldn’t tell how far. For us, Edom meant Rome. <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · four kingdoms; Rashi on Genesis 36:43</sub>
9. Rabbi Meir adds the invitation. God showed Jacob the empires rising and falling and said, אַף אַתָּה עוֹלֶה  “You, too, will ascend.” Jacob feared he would fall like the others, though God promised he would not. And Rabbi Meir concludes, לֹא הֶאֱמִין וְלֹא עָלָה  “He did not believe, and he did not ascend.” <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · Rabbi Meir</sub>
10. **The Sages ask the group to discuss:** Would you have climbed? What would have held you back?
   - *Teacher: Give pairs a few minutes, then hear from the room. Then tap to hear the Sages.*
   - Then the Sages answer: Rabbi Meir blames Jacob for not believing. But Jacob had just watched four empires rise and fall. Most of us fear the fall more than we want the height.
11. **The Sages:** Ask the Sages anything. Choose a question, or type your own. *(Typing is on: typed questions are matched to these.)*
   1. *Which reading do you prefer?* → We never ruled. Which one speaks to you, and why?
   2. *What do the rung numbers mean?* → They measure each empire’s rise before its fall: seventy for Babylon, fifty-two for Media, a hundred and eighty for Greece. Edom’s number was hidden, even from Jacob. <sub>Vayikra Rabbah 29:2 · four kingdoms</sub>
   3. *Why is Edom Rome?* → We lived under Rome, the empire that destroyed the Temple, and Esau’s other name became our name for it. Rashi, following an old midrash, says of one of Esau’s descendants, “Magdiel: this is Rome.” <sub>Rashi on Genesis 36:43</sub> / Esau’s own visit takes this up, if you have time.
   4. *Did Jacob institute the evening prayer?* → The Talmud says so: Abraham the morning prayer, Isaac the afternoon, and Jacob the evening, from “he encountered the place.” <sub>Berakhot 26b</sub>
   5. *What does it mean that God is “the Place”?* → That God contains the world, and the world does not contain God. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:9</sub>
   6. *Is prayer a ladder?* → A later teacher, the Ba’al HaTurim, counted it: סולם, ladder, spelled with a vav, equals קול, voice. The voice of the prayer of the righteous is a ladder for the angels to go up on. <sub>Ba’al HaTurim on Genesis 28:12 · ladder and voice</sub>
   7. *Why do sulam and Sinai match?* → We said, “The letters of this equals the letters of that.” Later teachers did the arithmetic: both come to 130. <sub>B’reishit Rabbah 68:12 · Sinai; Ba’al HaTurim on Genesis 28:12 · ladder and Sinai</sub>
12. **The rung:** “Would you have climbed?”
   - **Ask your child:** Would you have climbed? What would have held you back?
13. *Davar acher*: two more readers of the ladder, Rambam and Ramban, and Rashi, who built on us. *(Suggests: Rambam, Ramban, Rashi.)*
