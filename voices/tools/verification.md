- **CONFIRMED** = the source says what the brief says (a wording note may still apply). **PARTLY** = part of the claim is missing from the source, worded differently, or attributed differently. **NOT FOUND** = not in the source.
- References use Sefaria's numbering. Commentary comments are numbered within each verse, so Rashi on Genesis 28:11:4 is the fourth Rashi comment on 28:11. Talmud segments are numbered within the amud (Chullin 91b:11).

## What to correct before publishing

1. **Gen 28:17 (RJPS):** `Shaken` (not *"afraid"*), `abode of God` (not *"house of God"*), `gateway to heaven` (not *"gateway of heaven"*); the clause reads `and that is the gateway to heaven`. RJPS uses "house of God" only in its footnote explaining the name Bethel (28:19).
2. **Gen 28:22 (RJPS):** `I will set aside a tithe for You`; *"the tenth"* is not the RJPS wording. The stone `shall be God’s abode`.
3. **Gen 28:12 (RJPS):** `going up and down on it` and `reached to the sky`, not *"ascending and descending"* or *"heaven"*. `stairway` is right, with the footnote `Or “ramp”; in contrast to others “ladder.” Heb. sullam.`
4. **Gen 25:27 (RJPS):** `Jacob became a mild man who stayed in camp`. *"Dwelling in tents"* appears only as the literal sense in the footnote (`lit. “a sitter in tents.”`). Esau is `a skillful hunter`.
5. **Gen 27 wording (RJPS):** 27:34 `he burst into wild and bitter sobbing`; 27:41 `Let but the mourning period of my father come`; 27:44 `awhile` (one word); 27:45 `Then I will fetch you from there.`
6. **Gen 29:20 and 27:44:** the Hebrew echo (ימים אחדים) disappears in RJPS, which has `awhile` at 27:44 and `but a few days` at 29:20. Show the Hebrew if the lesson depends on the echo.
7. **Gen 28:13:** RJPS reads `And standing beside him was GOD` and adds a footnote that the label `may refer to an agent (in this case, one of the angels)`. The commentators in this packet read the same Hebrew (נצב עליו) as "over/above him" (Rashi, Chullin, Bereshit Rabbah) or "upon it", the ladder (Maimonides). In Sefaria's data that footnote also carries the wrong lemma (see Data issues).
8. **Chullin 91b:** the angels are `descending and gazing at his image below`. The Talmud does not say they found Jacob asleep; that detail is Bereshit Rabbah 68:12 (`descending below, and finding him asleep`).
9. **Vayikra Rabbah 29:2:** the rung numbers (Babylon 70, Media 52, Greece 180, Edom unknown) are Rabbi Shmuel bar Naḥman's. The invitation to climb, Jacob's fear and refusal, and the consequence are Rabbi Meir's, transmitted by Rabbi Berekhya, Rabbi Ḥelbo and Rabbi Shimon ben Yoḥai, and that version has no numbers. The petiḥta is opened by Rabbi Naḥman.
10. **Bereshit Rabbah 68:12:** the Sinai reading belongs to "the Rabbis"; the ladder-equals-Sinai letter equivalence is an anonymous "another matter"; the number 130 appears only in the Sefaria translator's footnote. The text does not say whether Rabbi Ḥiyya or Rabbi Yanai read "on it" as "on Jacob".
11. **Who taught that the Patriarchs instituted the prayers:** in Berakhot 26b it is Rabbi Yosei son of Rabbi Ḥanina (supported by a baraita), and Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi there ties the prayers to the daily offerings. Bereshit Rabbah 68:9 attributes the Patriarchs teaching to Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. Attribute according to the source you cite.
12. **Guide, Introduction:** on Sefaria the ladder passage is "Guide for the Perplexed, Prefatory Remarks 22–23" (Maimonides' own introduction, Hebrew heading פתיחת הרמב"ם). Sefaria's node "Part 1, Introduction" is a different section, and the version "Moreh Nevuchim, Ibn Tibon Introduction" contains only Samuel ibn Tibbon's translator's preface.
13. **Baal HaTurim:** Sefaria's "Ba'al HaTurim on Genesis" has nothing on chapter 28; the gematriot are in "Kitzur Ba'al HaTurim on Genesis" 28:12. It gives סולם = קול, סולם = ממון, and also סולם = עוני, and separately סלם = סיני. The numbers 130 and 136 are not written in the text (my arithmetic confirms them). No English translation exists in the export.
14. **Ramban 28:12 (Chavel's English):** the rung passage says Media went up `one hundred and eighty rungs` and leaves out Greece. Chavel's own note 12 and the Hebrew give Media 52 and Greece 180.
15. **Rashi English:** the "Sefaria Edition" translation has nothing on chapter 28, so the packet uses Rosenbaum–Silbermann (1929–34). Its English is dated ("thee", "whereon") and mixes the translators' parenthetical source citations into Rashi's text.

### 20. Guide, Introduction: CONFIRMED (the location differs from the brief)

The Sefaria reference is **Guide for the Perplexed, Prefatory Remarks 22–23**. That node is Maimonides' own introduction (Hebrew heading פתיחת הרמב"ם), which is often cited as the Introduction to Part 1. Paragraph 22 defines the two kinds of prophetic parable, and paragraph 23 gives the ladder as the example of the first kind.

**Guide for the Perplexed, Prefatory Remarks 22** (Friedlander, excerpt)
<!-- src: Guide for the Perplexed, Introduction (Sefaria: Prefatory Remarks 22-23) | en -->
> Know that the figures employed by prophets are of two kinds: first, where every word which occurs in the simile represents a certain idea;

**Guide for the Perplexed, Prefatory Remarks 23** (Friedlander, excerpt)
<!-- src: Guide for the Perplexed, Introduction (Sefaria: Prefatory Remarks 22-23) | en -->
> An example of the first class of prophetic figures is to be found in Genesis:—“And, behold, a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven; and, behold, the angels of God ascending and descending on it” (Gen. 28:12). The word “ladder” refers to one idea: “set up on the earth” to another: “and the top of it reached to heaven” to a third: “angels of God” to a fourth: “ascending” to a fifth; “descending” to a sixth; “the Lord stood above it” (ver. 13) to a seventh. Every word in this figure introduces a fresh element into the idea represented by the figure.

### 24. Chullin 91b: PARTLY

In the William Davidson English, **bold** is the translation of the Aramaic and unbolded words are Rabbi Steinsaltz's explanation.

The sun set early for him, and the land contracted (Rabbi Yitzḥak):

**Chullin 91b:6** (William Davidson English; bold = translation)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:6 | en_md -->
> **Rabbi Yitzḥak says:** The verse means that **the sun, which set** early exclusively **for him,** also **shone** early exclusively **for him** in order to rectify the disparity created by the premature sunset. The Gemara explains when the sun set early for him: **As it is written: “And Jacob went out from Beersheba and went toward Haran”** (Genesis 28:10). **And it is written** thereafter: **“And he encountered the place,** and he slept there, because the sun had set” (Genesis 28:11). **When** Jacob **arrived at Haran, he said:** Is it **possible** that **I passed a place where my fathers prayed and I did not pray** there? **When he set his mind to return, the land contracted for him. Immediately** the verse states: **“And he encountered the place,”** indicating that he arrived there miraculously.

**Chullin 91b:6** (Aramaic, vocalized)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:6 | he -->
> אָמַר רַבִּי יִצְחָק: שֶׁמֶשׁ הַבָּאָה בַּעֲבוּרוֹ זָרְחָה בַּעֲבוּרוֹ, דִּכְתִיב: ״וַיֵּצֵא יַעֲקֹב מִבְּאֵר שָׁבַע וַיֵּלֶךְ חָרָנָה״, וּכְתִיב: ״וַיִּפְגַּע בַּמָּקוֹם״. כִּי מְטָא לְחָרָן אָמַר: אֶפְשָׁר עָבַרְתִּי עַל מָקוֹם שֶׁהִתְפַּלְּלוּ אֲבוֹתַי וַאֲנִי לֹא הִתְפַּלַּלְתִּי? כַּד יָהֵיב דַּעְתֵּיהּ לְמִיהְדַּר, קְפַצָה לֵיהּ אַרְעָא, מִיָּד – ״וַיִּפְגַּע בַּמָּקוֹם״.

**Chullin 91b:7** (William Davidson English; bold = translation)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:7 | en_md -->
> **When he** had finished **praying** and **he wanted to return** to Haran, **the Holy One, Blessed be He, said: This righteous man came to my lodging place and he will depart without remaining overnight? Immediately, the sun set** before its proper time so that Jacob would stay overnight in that place.

**Chullin 91b:7** (Aramaic, vocalized)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:7 | he -->
> כַּד צַלִּי, בָּעֵי לְמֶיהְדַּר, אָמַר הַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא: צַדִּיק זֶה בָּא לְבֵית מְלוֹנִי, וְיִפָּטֵר בְּלֹא לִינָה? מִיָּד, ״בָּא הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ״.


The stones:

**Chullin 91b:8** (William Davidson English; bold = translation)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:8 | en_md -->
> The Gemara cites another exposition of Rabbi Yitzḥak to explain an apparent contradiction between two verses pertaining to this incident. **It is written: “And he took of the stones of the place,** and placed them under his head, and lay down in that place to sleep” (Genesis 28:11). **And it is written:** “And Jacob rose up early in the morning, **and he took the stone** that he had placed under his head, and set it up for a pillar, and poured oil upon the top of it” (Genesis 28:18). The first verse indicates that Jacob took several stones, whereas the latter verse indicates that he took only one stone. **Rabbi Yitzḥak says:** This **teaches that all those stones gathered to one place and each one said:** Let **this righteous man place his head upon me.** And it was **taught: And all of them were absorbed into one** large rock.

**Chullin 91b:8** (Aramaic, vocalized)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:8 | he -->

The angels and Jacob's image; God standing over him:

**Chullin 91b:11** (William Davidson English; bold = translation)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:11 | en_md -->
> It was **taught** that the angels were **ascending and gazing at the image of [bidyokeno]** Jacob **above,** engraved on the Throne of Glory, **and descending and gazing at his image below.** The angels subsequently became jealous of Jacob, and **wanted to endanger** his life. **Immediately** Jacob received divine protection, as the verse states: **“And behold, the Lord stood over him”** (Genesis 28:13). **Rabbi Shimon ben Lakish says: Were it not written** in a **verse it would be impossible to utter it,** in deference to God, since it describes God as standing over Jacob to protect him from the angels **like a man who waves** a fan **over his son** to cool him down.

**Chullin 91b:11** (Aramaic, vocalized)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:11 | he -->
> תָּנָא: עוֹלִין וּמִסְתַּכְּלִין בִּדְיוֹקְנוֹ שֶׁל מַעְלָה, וְיוֹרְדִין וּמִסְתַּכְּלִין בִּדְיוֹקְנוֹ שֶׁל מַטָּה. בְּעוֹ לְסַכּוֹנֵיהּ, מִיָּד ״וְהִנֵּה ה׳ נִצָּב עָלָיו״. אָמַר רַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן בֶּן לָקִישׁ: אִלְמָלֵא מִקְרָא כָּתוּב אִי אֶפְשָׁר לְאׇמְרוֹ, כְּאָדָם שֶׁמֵּנִיף עַל בְּנוֹ.


The land folded under him:

**Chullin 91b:12** (William Davidson English; bold = translation)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:12 | en_md -->
> The Gemara explains another verse from Jacob’s dream. “And behold, the Lord stood over him and said: I am the Lord, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac. **The land upon which you lie,** to you will I give it, and to your seed” (Genesis 28:13). The Gemara asks: **What is the greatness** of this promise, i.e., why is it expressed in this way despite the fact that in a literal sense Jacob was lying on a very small amount of land? **Rabbi Yitzḥak says:** This **teaches that the Holy One, Blessed be He, folded** up **the entirety of Eretz Yisrael and placed it under Jacob, our patriarch, so that it would be easy for his children to conquer.**

**Chullin 91b:12** (Aramaic, vocalized)
<!-- src: Chullin 91b:12 | he -->

- Also on this amud: the ladder's width (91b:9–10) and the wrestling angel (91b:13). The Jacob material begins on 91a and runs into 92a; the packet holds all of 91b.

### 25. Berakhot 26b: CONFIRMED

**Berakhot 26b:7** (William Davidson English; bold = translation)
<!-- src: Berakhot 26b:7 | en_md -->
> **Jacob instituted the evening prayer, as it is stated: “And he encountered [vayifga] the place and he slept there** for the sun had set” (Genesis 28:11). The word **encounter** means **nothing other than prayer, as it is stated** when God spoke to Jeremiah: **“And you, do not pray on behalf of this nation and do not raise on their behalf song and prayer, and do not encounter [tifga] Me** for I do not hear you” (Jeremiah 7:16). Jacob prayed during the evening, after the sun had set.

There is no English for these in the export. Working translation (mine, not a published translation; have it checked before students see it):
- 28:12:6: "Sulam" (סלם) in gematria equals "Sinai" (סיני), for He showed him the standing at Mount Sinai.
- 28:12:3: "Sulam" (סולם) in gematria equals "kol" (קול, voice): for the voice of the prayer of the righteous is a ladder for the angels to go up on; and so the angel went up in the flame of the offering. And prayer is the [Temple] service; therefore whoever concentrates in his prayer, the ladder is whole in its rungs and they can go up on it. "Sulam": in gematria "mamon" (ממון, money), and in gematria "oni" (עוני, poverty), for this one brings low and that one raises up.
- 28:12:2: "Sulam" (סולם) in gematria equals "zeh kisei ha-kavod" (זה כסא הכבוד, this is the Throne of Glory).

The text names the equivalences but never states the numbers. My arithmetic:

| Equivalence in the text | Values |
|---|---|
| סלם = סיני (28:12:6) | 60+30+40 = 130; 60+10+50+10 = 130. Equal. |
| סולם = קול (28:12:3) | 60+6+30+40 = 136; 100+6+30 = 136. Equal. |
| סולם = ממון (28:12:3) | 136; 40+40+6+50 = 136. Equal. |
| סולם = עוני (28:12:3) | 136; 70+6+50+10 = 136. Equal. |
| סולם = זה כסא הכבוד (28:12:2) | 136 vs. 130. Not equal as spelled here; it works for סלם (130). |

So your *"Sinai = 130?"* is right, and for *"136 = קול 'voice', or ממון 'money'?"* the answer is both, plus עוני (poverty). Among the other 28:12 comments, several work (חלום = זהו בנבואה, 84; והנה סלם = וקץ, 196; סלם מוצב = מרכבו, 268; סלם and סמל, and סלם and למס, are anagrams). Three do not add up as printed in this edition: והנה סולם מוצב = הכבש (340 vs. 327), והנה סלם = וזה מזבח העולה (196 vs. 191), and עלים [ויורדים] = בבל מדי יון ורומי (426 vs. 416). Check a printed edition before using those.
